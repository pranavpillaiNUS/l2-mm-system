\section{Deterministic Execution and Risk}

\subsection{The execution model defines the fill sample}

Arrival, cancellation, and queue rules determine which recorded trades can
fill a hypothetical order. They therefore determine both economic outcomes
and the conditioning set for the signal study. V3 uses \CurrentModel,
\code{market_data_before_private_actions_v1},
\code{post_response_proxy_depth_boundary_v1}, and the
\code{pause_until_snapshot} trade-gap policy. V2 economics remain labeled
\LegacyModel{} and are used only for an explicit historical comparison.

\subsection{Clock and order lifecycle}

Private actions strictly before the next market timestamp execute first. At
the same millisecond, all recorded depth/trade events execute before private
arrivals, unresolved market-data ties use depth before trade and preserve
within-stream order. Consequently a new order arriving at a trade's
millisecond cannot capture that trade, and a cancel arriving at the same
millisecond can lose the race. This is a deterministic policy for unresolved
timestamps, not knowledge of the venue's sub-millisecond sequence.

For submission at $t_s$, entry latency $L_e$, and seeded jitter $J_e$,
\begin{equation}
 t_a=t_s+L_e+J_e.
\end{equation}
For a cancel requested at $t_r$ with its own latency and jitter,
\begin{equation}
 t_c=\max(t_r+L_c+J_c,t_a).
\end{equation}
Pending entry contributes potential inventory but cannot fill. At arrival,
the simulator checks whether the order would cross. Post-only crossing orders
terminate with an explicit reason. Resting orders remain fillable until their
cancellation reaches the modeled exchange.

\begin{finding}
\textbf{A tested race.} A synthetic order is placed at 0 ms, arrives and joins
the queue at 5 ms, receives a cancel request at 6 ms, fills at 10 ms, and emits
\code{cancel_too_late} when the cancel arrives at 11 ms. The demo tests the
order lifecycle. It does not calibrate the latency assumption to a live venue.
\end{finding}

\subsection{External queue credit and own-order FIFO}

The simulator decomposes an order's modeled quantity ahead into external
displayed volume and remaining earlier own orders at the same side and price:
\begin{equation}
 Q_i^{\mathrm{ahead}}=Q_i^{\mathrm{external}}+Q_i^{\mathrm{own}}.
\end{equation}
The external queue starts from displayed quantity, with an ordering floor
imposed by earlier own orders. A depth decrease unexplained by recorded trades
can reduce only the external component:
\begin{equation}
 Q_{i,t+1}^{\mathrm{external}}
 =Q_{i,t}^{\mathrm{external}}(1-c\rho_t),\qquad c\in[0,1],
\end{equation}
where $\rho_t$ is the inferred fraction removed and $c$ the queue-cancellation
credit. At $c=0$, unexplained cancellations confer no advancement. At $c=1$,
the model gives proportional advancement. Neither endpoint is observed ground
truth, and they are not guaranteed mathematical bounds on actual execution.

Depth-decrease attribution waits until all trades at the same millisecond have
been observed. Otherwise depth-before-trade processing could mistake traded
quantity for cancellation and award improvement twice. Recorded aggressor
volume advances non-overlapping own FIFO ranks. The complete transition is
planned and validated before mutation, with the invariant
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i q_{i,t}^{\mathrm{maker\ fill}}
 \le q_t^{\mathrm{recorded\ aggressor}}.
\end{equation}
Cancelling an earlier own order releases its remaining own quantity from later
orders' ranks. A scale-aware Decimal tolerance of $10^{-24}$ handles division
residue. It cannot create fill volume. Partial fills preserve remaining order
quantity and FIFO relationships.

\subsection{Worst-case working exposure}

Inventory alone understates risk during delayed cancel/replace. Let $I_t$ be
current inventory and $B_t,S_t$ the remaining quantities of all buy and sell
orders that can still fill. These sets include active orders, pending entries,
and live orders with cancels in flight. The admission envelope checks the
appropriate prospective side against
\begin{equation}
 I_t+\sum_{i\in B_t}q_i\le I_{\max},\qquad
 I_t-\sum_{i\in S_t}q_i\ge-I_{\max}.
\end{equation}
Opposite-side working quantity is not assumed to offset a same-side fill
scenario. Breaching an invariant aborts replay rather than being recorded as a
temporary harmless overshoot. The experimental cap is 0.01 BTC, and each new
baseline quote is 0.001 BTC.

\subsection{Accounting, marks, and termination}

Let fill direction $s_i$ be $+1$ for a buy and $-1$ for a sell, quantity $q_i$,
price $p_i$, and fee $f_i$. With cash $C$ and inventory $I$,
\begin{equation}
 C\leftarrow C-s_i p_iq_i-f_i,\qquad I\leftarrow I+s_iq_i,
 \qquad V_t=C_t+I_tm_t.
\end{equation}
Maker/taker fee classification follows actual modeled execution. The baseline
uses post-only orders and produced no taker fills in V3, but the accounting
code still distinguishes the two paths. Reconciliation separates completed
lots from residual inventory so matched-lot statistics are not confused with
full-strategy marked P\&L.

A known data gap invalidates the complete timestamp group before it can drive
fills or strategy callbacks. Local order and queue state is invalidated until
snapshot recovery. This is fail-closed research bookkeeping. It does not assert
that exchange orders disappear on a feed failure. At the last market event,
private actions scheduled later are not executed against an invented future
book. Remaining live orders expire. Pending-action diagnostics are captured
before that final cleanup and can therefore be nonzero.

Each nominal five-hour result sums five independently initialized hourly
episodes. Inventory is marked at each hour's final mid and reset without
liquidation cost. The resulting statistic measures repeated short episodes,
not one continuous five-hour portfolio or deployable account return.
